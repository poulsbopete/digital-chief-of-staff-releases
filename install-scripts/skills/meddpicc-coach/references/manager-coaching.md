# Manager Coaching Playbook

Use this when the user is a frontline manager — not a rep coaching their own deal.

## The fundamental shift

| Rep coaching | Manager coaching |
|---|---|
| Unit of analysis: **one deal** | Unit of analysis: **one rep's pipeline** |
| Question: "How do I save this deal?" | Question: "What chronic gap is killing this rep's deals?" |
| Output: 3 next actions on this deal | Output: 1 coaching focus for the month |
| Time horizon: this week | Time horizon: next quarter |

## How to read a pipeline matrix

Given a grid of deals × MEDDPICC scores, ask three diagnostic questions in order:

### 1. What's the rep's chronic weakness?

Take the average score per element across all their deals. The weakest element is the rep's pattern. A 3.4 average on Metrics across 6 deals isn't bad luck — it's a discovery problem.

### 2. Is it a skill gap or a documentation gap?

This is the manager's most important diagnostic, because the coaching is completely different:

- **Documentation gap** — rep *knows* the info but doesn't write it down. Cheap to fix: change CRM hygiene expectations, require deal reviews to include written MEDDPICC notes.
- **Skill gap** — rep genuinely doesn't know. Expensive to fix: requires role-play, shadow calls, and probably 2-3 months of focused coaching.

**How to tell them apart:** Ask the rep to defend the weakest element verbally on their top 3 deals. If they can rattle off names, dates, dollar amounts they hadn't written down — documentation gap. If they hand-wave, can't name people, change subjects — skill gap.

### 3. Is the pattern team-wide or rep-specific?

If you have data on multiple reps and the same element is weak across all of them, you don't have a coaching problem — you have a process problem. Examples:

- **Whole team weak on Paper Process** → Your sales ops isn't preparing legal/security early enough. Fix the process, not the reps.
- **Whole team weak on Decision Criteria** → Your discovery template doesn't ask for it. Fix the template.
- **Whole team weak on Economic Buyer** → Your enablement isn't teaching reps how to access power. Fix enablement.

This is the highest-leverage realization a manager can have, and it's invisible without rollup data.

## The MEDDPICC-anchored 1:1

Default cadence — weekly 30-minute 1:1, structured like this:

**Minute 0-2 — Open with the pattern, not the pipeline.**
> "Your weakest element this week is [X] across [N] deals. Average score [Y]. Walk me through it on [deal A] first."

This avoids "walk me through your pipeline" — which produces rehearsed updates, not diagnostic conversations.

**Minute 2-15 — Drill on the pattern across 2-3 deals.**
Ask the same MEDDPICC question on different deals. If the rep can't answer it consistently, that confirms the pattern.

**Minute 15-25 — Pick ONE deal that needs movement.**
Stop diagnosing, start prescribing. What's the single move this week that closes the gap on this deal?

**Minute 25-30 — Commit and close.**
Rep states the move in their own words. You write it down. You'll open next week's 1:1 by asking what happened.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't ask "walk me through your pipeline."** Theater. Open with the pattern.
- **Don't coach more than one element at a time.** Reps can fix one habit at a time. Two is none.
- **Don't accept score inflation.** If the rep gives themselves a 7 on Champion, ask: "Name one thing Sarah did internally last week when you weren't in the room." If they can't, the score is 4.
- **Don't override scores without explanation.** Manager overrides on the AI scores are a coaching artifact — they should come with one sentence on why.
- **Don't coach the deal in the 1:1.** Coach the rep. The deal is downstream of the rep's habits.

## Manager 1:1 opener templates

Replace "walk me through your pipeline" with one of these:

- "Across your last 5 deals, your weakest element is [X]. Talk me through it on each one, starting with the biggest."
- "I want to focus on [element] this month. Pick the deal where you're strongest on it, and tell me what you did differently."
- "On [deal], your Champion score is a 4. Convince me you have a champion, not a coach."
- "If [biggest deal] goes south, what's the most likely reason? Score yourself on that element honestly."
- "You scored Pain an 8 on three deals this month. Tell me the compelling event on each. If they sound similar, we have a problem."

## The "calibration gap" — manager vs. AI scores

When a manager overrides the AI's scores, the *delta* is itself diagnostic:

- **Manager scores Champion lower than AI consistently** → The rep is calling friendly contacts "champions." Coach: define champion strictly, force the rep to name what each contact has done internally.
- **Manager scores Metrics higher than AI** → The rep knows more than they wrote down. Coach: documentation hygiene, not skill.
- **Manager scores EB lower than AI** → The rep is taking the champion's word for who the EB is. Coach: insist on direct EB contact before any 7+ score.

A 2-point delta is worth a conversation. A 4-point delta is worth a full coaching session.
